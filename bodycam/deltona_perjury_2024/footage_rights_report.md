**Legal basis:**
- U.S. federal works are public domain (17 U.S.C. §105), but that **doesn't apply** to state or local agencies.
- Florida: Art. I §24 Fla. Const. and Ch. 119 F.S. (public records). *Microdecisions, Inc. v. Skinner*, 889 So. 2d 871
  (Fla. 2d DCA 2004) held that a Florida public official can't claim copyright in public records unless the
  legislature specifically allows it. No statute gives VSO copyright in bodycam video. In practice, Florida agency
  bodycam releases are very widely treated as reusable. Caveat: the holding is from an intermediate appellate court
  about GIS maps. Applying it to video is a reasonable reading but hasn't been tested in court.
- Separate from copyright: **privacy/publicity and defamation**. The person shown has only been charged. See §4.

## 3. YouTube policy points to re-check before upload

The live pages must be re-read at export time, because policies change:
- Advertiser-friendly guidelines: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/6162278
- Violent or graphic content: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2802008
- Monetisation / reused content (YPP): https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392

Practical points: bodycam reuploads with no commentary get rejected as "reused content". This video needs
substantial original narration, analysis, legal explanation and editing. Strong profanity should be bleeped,
especially in the first 7–8 seconds. There's no graphic violence in this case. Blurring and narration **don't
guarantee** monetisation.

## 4. Defamation and fairness rules for this case

- He has been **charged**, not convicted (no disposition found as of 2026-11-28). Use "accused", "charged", "according to
  the Sheriff's Office".
- The released video is an **agency-edited** compilation, and the agency is a party to the dispute. Say so.
- Show the full context of what the footage shows. That includes the officer's profanity at 3:49 and the driver
  saying "this arm is broken". Don't edit out things that cut against the Sheriff's version.
- Blur the driver's face, the business phone number on his shirt, plates, the home exterior and the case/ID numbers.
